*From the entry "His answer of 2026-11-21" (in `square.md`):*

  - **"Nobody starved" is said only of a run that printed the band's states, and then it means: nobody died, nobody's state was worded starving.** A run's log names a starving member only on a `who:` line (`--who`), and the game words a state "starving: ..." only of someone at work with an empty belly: on the way to the pot, asleep or fending, an empty belly is a `who:` line at 0 % fed with its place printed, no word. Without `--who` only the bedless show (the food line's "without one: NAME (state)") and a death ("NAME has died of hunger"; the count of the living). So: **the three site scripts run `--who --log-hours 1`, fail on a death or the word, and count the empty bellies and say them** (`'^  who: [A-Za-z]*, *0 % fed: .*(at X Y Z)$'`; `$S/belly.py LOG...` does the same with more; it and the other measuring scripts of 2026-11-21 are kept in `~/CASTLE_GAME/releases/wip/scripts/`). **An empty belly for an hour before a meal is ordinary in every build** (his own square: 111 member-hours over fifteen sites and fifty days): compare counts, never claim none. A whole round's "nobody starved" was retracted on 2026-11-21, and its first recount too, which had counted the word. Today's square costs more of them than his build's (twelve days at fifteen sites 85 for 21; the eight heaviest 144 for 36: the first night, the hearth and the first log later; a set run with `--square-10` alone is ten metres with today's walls, not his build's square, which is `--square-10 --square-walls-inside --no-fire-ground`): what should fail a gate, and whether the hearth should come before the whole floor, are put to him.

*From the entry "Building and zoning, a pass after stage 6a" (in `housing.md`):*

  - **The band's pace is one number** (`Castle.Colony.Serf_Pace`, 1.5; `Set_Pace`, `--pace`): `Work_Rate` and `Walk` are multiplied by it and nothing else is. Never put it in `Vigour` (`Practise` goes by that: learning would quicken) or in `Castle.Nav.Walk_Speed` (the herds, the walkers and `path_check` go by that). **Pace 1 is the band every figure in the documents before 2026-11-12 was measured at**: the seven older reference runs are held at `--pace 1`, and `colony_check` starts every section at it through a `Reset` of its own (a new section that is about timing at today's pace says `Set_Pace (Serf_Pace)`). Nothing was retuned for the pace: John's to ask for.
  - **A fender is not a gatherer** (`Go_Forage` with `M.Fends`, `Stake_Weight`): the nearest of bush, apple tree and vegetables by the way to it alone. A gatherer's rules (vegetables alone while none are stored, `Veg_Pull`, the way home counted) are John's of stage 4 for gatherers and stand. Changing who is where in autumn moves the first winter of every run in which somebody fends: five reference logs were recorded again for it.
  - **Three new systems, three switches, each giving the band as it was exactly**: `Set_Cook_Hold` (`--no-cook-hold`), `Set_Curing` (`--no-curing`), `Set_Ladders` (`--no-ladders`). The seven older reference runs are held at `--no-beds --pace 1 --no-cook-hold --no-curing` (no housing in them: ladders do not matter), and the eighth with the three off is the log kept before the round. `colony_check`'s own `Reset` sets all three off (with pace 1): a new section that is about one of them says so (`Set_Up (With_Curing => True)`, `Set_Ladders (True)`, `Set_Cook_Hold`).
  - **The cook keeps a pot's fill** (`Cook_Hold`, `Cook_Keeps` 900 points, whatever is in the pot): raw eaters have a limit of their own (`Raw_Free`) and `Food_In_Store` goes by the same number, or a serf leaves its work for food it may not take. Only with a standing hearth and a living cook. The cook's own `Usable` is untouched. **What a hungry band waits for on a first day is mostly the pot's filling** (`Think_Cook` does not stir until the pot is full or nothing more is to be fetched: three hours on the reference band's first day; a stage 4 rule, measured and reported, not changed: his to ask for).
  - **Meat is in pounds, and three tables are functions** (`Carry_Limit`, `Cell_Limit`, `Stew_Food`: by `Curing_On` for meat; the tables themselves are `Carry_Table`, `Cell_Table`, `Stew_Table`). `Meat_Of` is a deer's dressed weight; what is kept of it is `Kept_Of (lb, salt)`. **Nothing spoils**: salt's whole effect is at the butchering (all kept, or a quarter). Spoilage is stage 11's; don't build it into this.
  - **Salt is fetched, never asked for**: a hunter with empty hands, before it hunts, a sackful from the cart to a stockpile while less than `Salt_Keep_Lb` is stored (`Go_Cart`, `To_Cart`, `At_Cart`). Salt and ladders have no maker in the band: `Asked_For` is False for both with the demand chain off, and nothing gives either a level with it on.
  - **A load in its arms is taken where it was going before it eats** (`Load_First` in `Think`; `Topping_Up` stops when it is hungry with one): not for one that fends, one below `Very_Hungry`, one with nowhere to put it (`Can_Store`: `Go_Store`'s own test, the whole load in one cell; one more item fitting is not enough), or food of its own gathering with no stew to be had. `Sees_To_Needs` is asked first in `Think`: anything new it does with a serf's hands goes through `Load_First`. `colony_check` looks at every meal of three days.

- Stage 5b's rules (skills; 2026-11-10; `docs/production/briefs/Stage_5b_brief.md`, the nine) stand as built. They were drawn from John's design documents with first values of mine and put to him as "agree, or change any"; after trying the build he answered "I only have critiques that don't concern functionality that we can tweak later, the important part is getting the structure in place and working. So 5c it is." So they are accepted as the structure, not ruled on line by line: don't treat a value of them as settled against him, and don't change one unasked. In short: five working skills (gathering, forestry, hunting, portering, cooking) and six trade skills that only grow alongside; a level changes the pace of skilled work only (0.75 with none, the old pace a third of the way, 1.5 at mastery), a hunter's aim and a cook's pot; people arrive unequal; aptitude (0.5 to 1.5, for life) is how fast each learns each; an hour on the job, the whole trip, is an hour of practice in the work's skill, with fractions to the skills beside it (`Learns`), by the work done, not the job held; the player sees everyone's levels and aptitudes; nothing is gated by skill yet. Standing by default, since John was asked and took up neither: the band's first cook is whoever has the best cooking aptitude (not the best level), and a member whose job nobody gave it is directed by no one. In the code: every work timer goes through `Work_Rate (M, Work_Kind)` (condition x skill x direction; `Handling` for what has no skill in it), never `Vigour` alone, except walking and the hunter's patience; a new kind of work is a `Work_Kind` with its row in `Learns`; skills-off (`--no-skills`) must keep giving stage 5a's reference log line for line, so a change to the colony that is not about skills is checked against that (`tools/reference_check.sh`).

- Designer's rules for stage 4 (2026-11-08), the theme of the game: serfs are self-reliant. Hunger ticks down and a serf won't let itself starve. The campfire is a zone (7 x 7 m, the fire in the middle, logs round it for seats, the band sleeps round it); the iron pot over it they brought with them. Anyone can eat raw food (fruit, berries, vegetables) but it fills poorly; the cook makes stew in the pot (meat and vegetables; berries when the vegetables are gone, then fruit), which goes much further, and serfs take stew over raw. A gatherer's priority is wild vegetables: one score of distance, demand and stock (a tad further for vegetables, further when demand is high, further still when the stock is low, vegetables alone when there are none stored). Starving with no food to be had, serfs leave their job to fend for themselves (production stalls) and confidence falls; in winter, with none at all, they die. Mood is per serf; the player sees only the band's average (confidence), never a nag per person. A cook, a hunter or two and a gatherer or two should feed a starting band with a small surplus (balance later). Theft, spoilage and leaving come later. Grain is a by-product of porters cutting straw (designer's call, 2026-11-09), so gatherers aren't overloaded; the cook uses it last.
