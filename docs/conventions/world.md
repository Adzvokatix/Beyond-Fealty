# Conventions: The world: the ground kept, the whole map held, water, falls, trees

What past sessions learned and John ruled about this part, for whoever changes it. Read it with `docs/systems/world.md`, `docs/systems/water.md`.

Moved whole from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-22: every line is as it stood there, in the order it stood in. A line in italics says which entry the lines under it were parted from. Where an entry says "on branch `stockpile`, not merged", read "merged 2026-11-22". New rules of this part are added here, at the top, dated.

- The whole map held (2026-11-14 and 15; `docs/handoffs/Handoff_2026_11_15_whole_map.md` has his words, what was measured, built, proved and his calls; `docs/systems/world.md`, "The whole map held"; `colony.md`, "The colony's own ground"; `rendering.md`, "The far land"). Accepted and merged: on `main`, the far land off unless asked for. In the code:
  - **Measure with everything loaded before letting anything be loaded.** The runner with every stack in took 85 s for the band's 5.5: with nothing unloaded to stop it, a goal out of reach was looked for over the whole map. What was loaded had bounded the colony's index, its searches and its routes; nothing said so anywhere. Anything that has only ever run on a little ground gets run on all of it first (`colony_run --ground-stacks 400 --no-mesh --nav-stats`).
  - **The colony reads its own ground and nothing beyond, whatever else is loaded** (`Castle.Colony.Set_Ground_Stacks (N)`, told by the host: the stacks within N of the stake's; N is `Integer (Ground_Reach_M / 8) + 5`, never fewer than were in, in the runner and in the game alike). **One function asks whether a stack is in for the band's own doing, `Own_V`**: nothing else of the band may call the world's version reader. Its routes take `Within => Own_Region` (`Castle.Nav.Within_Stacks`); a new `Castle.Nav.Plan` in the colony takes it too. **What the player plans is not the band's doing** and is read from all the ground that is held (three places that each say so: a path's line shown and counted, the fill of housing inside a round of paths); a new planner of the player's reads so too, and a new reader of the band's does not. So **a run is the same run whatever its host holds**: prove a change to what the colony reads by the eight logs with none recorded again, the stress states against `main`'s, and `colony_run --ground-stacks 400` ending in the usual run's state (`tools/stress.sh runner` has `crowd_whole_map`).
  - **The band's reach covers every zone** (`Ground_Reach_M`: `Search_Reach`, `Path_Far`, `Housing_Far`, and `Stores_Far` for a stockpile or the camp, up to 256 m): with its routes kept to its own ground, a zone the reach does not cover is never served. A new kind of zone, or a new place the band is sent, goes into the reach.
  - **The game holds every stack's blocks and streams the meshes alone** (`Stream` in `game/castle_game.adb`). `States` is the mesh's state; whether a stack's blocks are held is `Held` (its ground version). A stack out of view is asked for without a mesh (`Castle.Streaming.Request (..., Mesh => False)`, results in a ring of their own: `Poll (..., Meshed => False)`), and a stack that leaves the view keeps its blocks. **What is held and not drawn is made again when its edits have moved** (`Hold_More` looks the map over all the time; `Held_Gen`, `Hold_Stale` after a load): anything new that changes blocks without moving the edits' generation, or that replaces the edits wholesale as a load does, must say so there, or a held stack goes stale where nobody is looking. `--no-whole-map` is the old way. Prove a change to it with the scripted probes (`--save-at F PATH`, `--load-at F PATH`, `--probe-block X Y Z`, `--hold-wait`, `--mem`): a dig left 520 m behind must read as dug, and a save from before it loaded while away must read as earth.
  - **It costs about a gigabyte: the game holds 1.5 to 1.6 GB** (548 MB the old way), under his ceiling of 4. The whole map is held 12 s after the first frame on 31 workers, the main thread giving it half a millisecond a frame at most; not run on a machine of few processors.
  - **Nothing asked of `Castle.Streaming` is lost any more**: its queue has room for every stack twice and a worker with a result waits while the ring is full. Before, it dropped jobs past 32,768 and results past 8,192, silently (the runner's "crawl" when asked for every stack at once: on `main` then it never ended).
  - **Released water no longer meets a wall where the loaded ground ended** (a lake let out at a view of 16 m piled 194 m3 against the edge; held, 28.6 m3 as at any view). How far and how fast it runs is his rule and untouched.
  - **The far land was off unless asked for, and is out of the game since 2026-11-16** (the far view's entry above; `Castle.Far`, `Far_Program`): the whole world as one coarse sheet under the land as it was made, and with it a thinner haze at the view's edge. He tried it and set it aside. **Without it the picture was `main`'s to the pixel with `--no-whole-map`**; with the whole map held some 870 scattered pixels differ, and `main` differs from itself by the same pixels when its first frames take longer (not looked into): compare pictures with the clock stopped, no shadows, and `--no-whole-map` on both sides, or after the map is held on neither. A view with snow falling differs between two runs of one build: use lying snow under a clear sky.

- What the ground costs to keep (2026-11-14; `docs/handoffs/Handoff_2026_11_14_ground_memory.md` has his words, what was counted, built and not taken, and his calls; `docs/systems/world.md`, "What a stack costs to keep" and "Boulders"). Accepted and merged: on `main`; its calls he did not speak of stand as built, first values still. Say a thing to him by what it does, never by a label of mine. In the code:
  - **His ceiling is 4 GB for the game** (comfortable on a machine of 8). The game holds about 510 MB at its default view. Say what a thing costs in memory against that when it is more than a few megabytes, **and measure it in the game**: two builds at the same view in a hidden window (`env -u WAYLAND_DISPLAY xvfb-run -a ./bin/castle_game ...`, reading the game's own `VmRSS` once the view has filled, not its wrapper's): the totals are the software renderer's, the difference is the game's (62 MB for the two storage changes at the default view). The stress table's memory column is a peak and shows none of it.
  - **Measure what the ground costs with the runner**: `colony_run --ground-stacks N` (400: the whole map, 65,536 stacks; in batches), `--mem` (what is kept of every stack in, counted by part from object sizes and allocations: `Castle.World_Blocks.Sizes`, `Castle.Chunks.Sizes_Of`), `--no-mesh`. A run with more ground in **was** another run until 2026-11-15 (the colony read whatever was loaded); now it must end in the very state of a run on the colony's own ground (the entry above; `crowd_whole_map` in `tools/stress.sh runner`). Derive nothing from the stress table's memory column: I did, got 39 KB a stack for 68, and had to take it back.
  - **A stack's ground summary is kept small** (`Castle.Water.Small_Summary`: a place of 16 bits a column; a plain column is its floor's row, any other a record in a pool of words). It is read and written a column at a time through `Castle.World_Blocks` (`Column_At`, `Put_Column`): **nothing may index a kept summary directly**, and `Summary_Of` hands back one shared buffer that the next call overwrites. Whether a stack's ground is known is one test (`Has_Ground`).
  - **A chunk's fine bricks are kept small** (`Castle.Chunks`: a record of words in the chunk's pool: how many different blocks, those blocks, which of them each place is). **A packed stack is read through `Block_In` (and `Fine_At`) only**, and is never written in place: an edited stack is packed again by a worker. A fine brick may hold any 64 blocks: over sixteen different it is written whole.
  - **Both are storage, and each has its switch** (`Small_Summaries`, `Small_Bricks`; `colony_run --full-summaries`, `--whole-bricks`; lines in `tools/stress.sh runner`). The debug build proves them as it goes: every summary kept and every column written is unpacked and compared with the whole, every fine brick packed is read back against the blocks it was made of. **After touching either, bring the whole map in on the debug build** (`./bin/debug/colony_run ... --ground-stacks 400 --no-mesh`, half a minute) **and hold the eight logs line for line with no log recorded again.**
  - **The generator's skin stays 2 m.** At 1 m it met his 4 KB and two 41-day runs ended in the same states, but the colony read below the skin more often (23 blocks for 15): it changes what a reader can be told. Put to him, not built; with the bricks small it would save half a kilobyte a stack.
  - **Boulders go by the mountain mask** (`Castle.Flora`: `Boulders_Open` 1.3, `Boulders_Mountain` 0.2, `Boulder_Foot` 0.2; generator 14). `flora_stats` counts them by the mask: run it before and after any change to what stands on the land, and compare each reference run's scene (the lines before "the band:") before recording a log again, since a boulder is solid. **A demo script films a moment of one history**: after a change to the world or to what the band does, find its moment again with `colony_run --who` (which says where each herd is, whether in flight, and who is nearest) or say the demo no longer shows it.
  - **Figures in the system documents say their generator.** Those of 13 were not all taken again on 14 (the handoff lists what was).

- Falls thrown from the lip (2026-11-11; `docs/handoffs/Handoff_2026_11_11_falls.md` has his words; `docs/systems/water.md`, "Falls"). Accepted as built: on `main`. First values, his to tune; don't treat one as settled against him, and don't change one unasked. In the code:
  - **It is a drawing.** `Castle.Falls` reads the simulation's flows (`Castle.Water.Outflows`, added for it: per face, the top stretch's, unclamped) and writes nothing back: the water still runs down the face a column a step, his water rules and `water_sim_check` are as they were, nothing is saved and the colony knows nothing of it. Water that lands where it is thrown ("reach") would change how far released water runs on every steep slope: his to ask for, not something to slip in.
  - **Never draw from one tick's flow.** On a slope a step's speed swings between a tenth and ten times its mean from tick to tick; `Castle.Falls` settles flow and depth over about a second (`Settle`) and throws at their ratio. What a real cut gives is 1 to 2 m/s at the lip; a head of a metre on made-up ground gives 5 to 6, which is why `Throw_Most` is there.
  - **A column has one more value in its texel** (`Surface_Texel.Air`: the sheet's top and thickness) and a stack under a sheet has a second cell to each column (z = 1 in the quad), six faces: the water pass culls back faces, and a fall is looked up at from its pool, so anything of water that can be seen from below needs its underside drawn.
  - **Only a stack a sheet stands over carries those cells** (`Air_Wanted` in the game; it is meshed again when a sheet first comes over it, once, and keeps them until it is unloaded). A band's ground is simulated wherever it has dug: the cells on every simulated stack cost 0.06 ms in the rain view with no water anywhere. Cost a water change with a band's ground in view, not only at the lake.
  - The tunables: what is thrown and how far at the top of `castle-falls.ads`, the sheet's look (`Fall_White`, `Fall_Streak`, `Fall_Alpha`) at the top of `castle-render.ads`. `--nofalls` is the look before, `--throw S` the share of the lip's speed; `tools/demo_falls.sh` has the two cuts at the mountain lake that it was tuned on (a `--frames` run never repeats a fall exactly: thin water pulses, so compare by eye or over a recording, not pixel by pixel).
  - Generated falls (a stream's own) are curtains still, asleep or awake: `Outflows` gives nothing between source water and source water, so that a woken stream does not change its look. Throwing them from the generated current is his call.

*From the entry "The last pass before stage 6: models drawn rounded, the bow and the axe, foliage, the herds' flight, the fringe of paths" (in `rendering.md`):*

  - **Trees, grass and the quiver came the same day, from his answer** ("we can rewrite the trees, grass can get a slight overhaul. A quiver would be nice, full animations are not important, we want a happy balance between realism and immersion, so just enough realism to be immersive without being expensive and time consuming"). Read that last as a standing rule: a thing should look like what it is and sit where it belongs, cheaply; no reach, draw or hand-over animation is owed; say what a look costs (GPU a frame at the four fixed views, quads over the view) before calling it done.
  - **A tree's shape** (`Castle.Flora.Draw_Tree`, generator 13; `docs/systems/world.md`, "Trees") goes by its seed and by where its blocks are in the world, nothing else, because every stack it reaches into draws it alone; stays within `Tree_Reach` of its trunk; and puts no bark off the trunk within a serf's height of the ground (bark is solid: the walking map changes), an apple's low boughs excepted. `Tree_At` (where a tree stands, its kind, its trunk) is what the colony goes by: reshape freely in `Draw_Tree`, but a change there moves the band's wood and every reference log. A stack draws every trunk before any bough (`Tree_Part`).

- Designer's rules for the world (2026-11-08): use the palette (`Castle.Colors`); dense forests in patches, a bit more spread out, with the open land between dotted by lone trees, which are the fruit (apple) trees since they need more sunlight; about half the berry bushes of before; trees vary in bark (aspen's silver) and in foliage colour and style.

- Designer's call (2026-11-08): felling a tree takes bites out of neighbouring crowns of the same leaves, and that stays: a falling tree takes foliage with it. Don't "fix" it. (A neighbour's trunk is kept whole; that one is a fix.)

- John's rules for water (2026-11-10; `docs/systems/water.md`, "John's rules", has his words): generated water is source water, asleep or awake. Asleep it holds its level. A block dug out beside it wakes it: the level drops beside the cut, less the further off, and where the drop reaches resting source water (`Wake_Drop`) that wakes too. A stream on a hill must not be overrun by the water above it. A block placed in water takes its place (nothing climbs onto it), and source water filled in to its level is source water no longer. A flow once established may sleep. Water is drawn stepped, not smoothed. They were built from his message and put to him with the build; he accepted it as built the same day ("Everything is working as it should, I am not seeing any room for improvement right now, I am happy with the state of water, all my tests achive the desired result."): first values, his to tune; don't treat one as settled against him, and don't change one unasked. Dams and rerouting are scrapped ("not the purpose of this game"). In the code: source water is simulated as how far it stands from its level (`Column.Rest`, from `Castle.Geology.Water_Level` through the stack summary); between two source columns the head is the difference of their drops, never of their heights (done-test: `water_sim_check`'s river section holds "nothing in, nothing out" on a sloping bed); source water goes out to other ground only across a face one of whose columns is `Touched` (the world as made holds its water: don't remove this, or every fall leaks once woken); every edit calls `Castle.Water.About_To_Change` before the ground summary changes and `Ground_Changed` after. Not built, and not owed: reach (water moving further than the next column in a step, his idea for momentum), sleep for water tumbling down a slope. **How far and how fast released water runs is as he wants it for now** (2026-11-11, after taking water down a mountain by reservoirs in stages: "which is what we want we don't want a players computer locking up becuase they cut a hole to the bottom of the lake and all the water started gushing into the valley, we could let the water travel farther faster later"): don't lengthen or quicken it unasked; the dials are listed in `docs/systems/water.md`, "How far released water runs". When he tells of a test of his, record his words and no more: don't write down what the system must have done to give it (I did, once, and had it backwards).
