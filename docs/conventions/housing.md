  - **Housing is a strip along a path's run** (`Plan_Housing_Lot`): the stretches a path is recorded as, end to end in a line, are one run (`Run_Of`: a stretch's joint is not an end or a turn); the strip's depth is `Housing_Deep_M` times the sum of the run's two parts on the grid. Its ground is what can be reached from its own path's edge without crossing a path (a fill within the strip: without it a strip took ground on the far side of another path). `--housing X0 Y0 X1 Y1` means begun at the first point and pulled to the second.
  - **"Fits a hovel" is `Place_House`'s verdict**, never a formula: `colony_check` zones 28 m beside a path at every angle, on the flat and on ground falling 2 m in 7, and wants the next two hovels in turn on either side by itself on the flat, three on the two sides together on the hillside; change the strip's depth, `Max_Push_B`, the yard or a hovel's size and run it. **Nothing is passed over** (second round, below): the count ends at the first hovel that does not fit.
  - **Zones can be combined, and a zone's number can then change** (`Join_Plan`): the runs of every zone are packed again and the zones after one that is gone move down. Three things keep a zone's number (a stockpile's places, `Camp_Zone_N`, a house): anything new that keeps one is renumbered there.
  - **Counting a strip's hovels costs five times its ground** (3.8 ms against 0.7 for 40 m): whatever shows a pull every frame calls `Plan_Housing (..., Count => False)`.
  - **A site is read from the world, column by column** (`Plan_Of`, `Work_In`, `Site_Patches`): its level, and a wall of stone where what is beside it stands more than `Retain_Over_B` over or under. Each house walls its own site as it finds the ground beside it; nothing of a wall is kept but what it still wants of stone as last counted (`Stone_Left`, `Note_Stone`).
  - **Earth that stands where stone is to go stays until the stone is laid in its place** (`Level_Column` digs only above a column's top; `Wall_Patch` swaps). The first version dug a wall's place out and built it after, and a builder who had dug the stair to his own door away with no stone to build it of starved in the pit. Anything else that removes ground a serf stands on or walks over must leave a way at every moment; `colony_check` plans a way from the builder to the stake every second of a build.
  - **No yard a serf can stand in behind a wall over its head** (`Apron_B` is two blocks: one open, one the wall). The walking map lets a serf drop a metre and climb half of one, so ground a metre or less under what is beside it, with no way out on its own level, is a pit: a builder daubing a back wall from a yard a metre wide starved in one. `colony_check` plans a way back to the stake from every spot round the hovels of two made-up hillsides that can be got to (it fails with a yard of four blocks). Anything that cuts ground down beside higher ground gets that check.
  - **`Top_By`, not `Ground_Row`, for the ground beside a site**: up while there is ground over ground, so an eave or a bough over a column is not its top, and nothing of a house is ground (`Is_Ground`).
  - **Stone is the third thing carried to a house**, before anything else of it, and the camp's hearth has first call (`Free_For_Building`). A fourth is another kind in `Kind_Of_Block`, its counts in the blob, and its place in the order.
