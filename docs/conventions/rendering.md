# Conventions: Drawing: the far view, the sky, shadows and the fire, models, cloth, cleared roofs

What past sessions learned and John ruled about this part, for whoever changes it. Read it with `docs/systems/rendering.md`.

Moved whole from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-22: every line is as it stood there, in the order it stood in. A line in italics says which entry the lines under it were parted from. Where an entry says "on branch `stockpile`, not merged", read "merged 2026-11-22". New rules of this part are added here, at the top, dated.

- **2026-11-24, the voxel people, merged on his word** (`docs/handoffs/Handoff_2026_11_23_voxel_people_drawn.md` has every round of it, his words whole; `docs/systems/people.md`, "The reference body" and "Voxel parts"; `docs/systems/rendering.md`, "Serfs of voxel parts"):
  - **A serf is voxel parts on its bones, one rigid part a bone, made when its look is made** (3.4 ms; the game makes at most one again a frame). Whatever moves a serf moves its bones (`Castle.Poses`, `Castle.Serfs`), never its voxels. `--no-reference-body --no-voxel` gives the serfs as they were before them.
  - **A change to the body is proved on the old path to the bit**: with the reference body off, a hash of 72,000 rigs and skins over the skin's first 64 rows, the texels past them counted (the tool, how to build it in a scratch copy, and its figures: `~/CASTLE_GAME/releases/voxel_people_2026_11_24/tools/README.txt`). A field added to `Castle.Bodies.Box` is named in every aggregate of one (`Add` in `castle-bodies.adb`, `castle-deer.adb`, `castle-props.adb`; the compiler names any left out) and stays out of the hash only where the old path never sets it (counted: `Round_Ends`, on none of 72,000).
  - **The head's eyes lie near its side faces**: with the head's upright edges rounded two pixels, or its jaw narrowed much past `Form_Jaw_Taper` (0.16), the eyes' outer columns (the whites) fall onto its sides. Look at a change to the head's shape in voxel dumps drawn flat, every face (`serf_check --voxels FILE N JOB`, then `serfdraw.py`), before a still. `Box.Round_Ends` rounds a box's top and bottom edges alone, for the voxel parts (the box path rounds every edge by `Round`).
  - **His size words on a look are voxels**: "a square", "a block", "a step" are 2.5 cm, half a body pixel.
- **2026-11-22, his notes after the merge** (`docs/handoffs/Handoff_2026_11_22_playtest_notes.md` has his words whole and each fault as it was reproduced):
  - **A hand's place is given in the frame of the part of the body it is held before, not in the world's** (the eaters' hands: `Castle.Poses.Chest_Frame`; "serfs arms and hands are behind there back when they sit down to eat"). A place given as so far before the hips is inside the chest as soon as the trunk leans, and the solver then does something that is right for a reach and wrong here. **Print the joint, the target and the solved middle joint in the body's own frame before calling an arm's pose a cause** (a four-line print in `Solve` showed the target inside the chest and the elbow behind the back; the stills alone looked like arms flung up). Look at a pose from behind and from the front, standing as well as in the motion he named: standing eaters had the same fault and he had named the seated ones.
  - **What a cloth is kept off is made from the boxes drawn, not written beside them** (`Dressed`, the sleeper's cloak; "just add their clothes to the "clip box", so the cloak wraps around it when they sleep"). The ten capsules written by hand had none along the ribs at all, and their radii were a body's, not its clothes': look at the list for what it does not cover before giving a thing a capsule of its own (a capsule for the forearms had been tried in 2026-11-13 and "changed nothing seen": the chest was the hole). **A worn cloak keeps the ten**: he called that one fine, and a change to what a worn cloth is kept off is a change to how every cloak hangs and to what 48 of them cost. **Clothes of cloth were tried at his word and dropped** (`docs/systems/people.md`, "Clothes of cloth"; the patch and stills in `~/CASTLE_GAME/releases/wip/cloth_skirts_trial_2026_11_22/`): a trial he leaves to me is judged by figures fixed beforehand (what it costs for thirteen, how it looks standing, seated and asleep beside what is there), stays in a scratch tree, and its patch and stills are kept where he can see them.
  - **A look he asks for in a word ("a bit more "form"") is built as first values behind a switch that gives the old look to the box, and shown beside the old one close up and at the distance the game is played from** (`Castle.Bodies.More_Form`, `--no-form`). **Add boxes and change how a box is drawn (its `Round`, its `Taper`) before moving a joint**: the head stood a pixel higher made every serf 5 cm taller than its genome says (`serf_check` holds a serf to its height), and the neck's pixel is taken from the chest alone, so that the hips and all that is placed from them stay. What a new box looks like on a body that bends is seen in a row at work (`--lineup 7 --lineup-does chop`), not standing: a belt that looked right standing was a platter round the waist of everyone chopping.
  - **A body of two boxes on two bones is one piece only if whoever places the bones keeps them a fixed length apart** (the deer's hindquarters and forequarters: `Castle.Animals.Whole_Back`; "deer can split in half when running up steep terrain"). Each end stood at its own height over its own ground. **Count it, since a still of a moving animal shows little**: `--deer-stats` says how far apart the two were drawn over a run (on a walk up the limestone hillside north-east of the reference stake 378 of 400 frames were over 1.1 backs' lengths and the furthest 2.62; mended, never over 1.00), and `deer_check` has a made-up hillside and cliff for it (`Castle.Serfs.Set_Ground_Source`: the first ground a check can give the feet). Where the ground is steeper than the body can pitch, the higher end keeps its own ground and the lower is held up: clamping both about the middle sank the uphill end into the hill and sat the deer down.

- The far view (2026-11-15 to 17; `docs/handoffs/Handoff_2026_11_15_far_view.md`, `Handoff_2026_11_16_far_view_haze_and_shadows.md` and `Handoff_2026_11_16_haze_horizon_boulders.md` have his words, the counts and his calls; `docs/systems/rendering.md`, "The far view"). Accepted and merged in three rounds: on `main`, on unless `--no-lod` (`c8f1c40` is the way back from the third). First values of mine but for his two figures (a haze 1 m deep, the map's rim over 12 m): his to tune, don't treat one as settled against him, and don't change one unasked. In the code:
  - **The far view is the world itself at coarser blocks** (`Castle.Lod`: a tile of 32 x 32 cells of 2^level blocks, made from the very stacks the game makes with the edits laid on, reduced by halves, meshed into the mesher's own quads). Never give it a second description of the world (that was the far land's fault: flat, no trees, nothing built). `./bin/lod_stats` counts it: run it before changing the reduction (`Parent`), the levels or the rings.
  - **A tile is drawn by the land's own shader**, at 2^level times the size (the per-draw origin's fourth number is the level; `v_lod` in the shaders). Anything in the land's shader that goes by the block must hold for a cell (the corner occlusion is across the cell). Tiles cast into the sun's maps since the second round (the far shadows, below); they are in no fire's map and no water's surface texture.
  - **Which level a pixel shows is the fragment shader's rule, one place** (the top of the land's `main`): the level of its ring about the focus, or the finest coarser one loaded there (`u_far_have`: a byte a level for every 16 m, how far in the tile is; the stacks' flags for level 0), rings cross-faded over `Far_Fade` and a tile coming in over `Far_Arrive_Frames`, both by the pixel's place in a fixed pattern, **never by the frame**. The CPU only leaves out tiles that cannot show (`Draw`: past their ring, out of the view, or wholly under finer ground that is loaded); it must never leave out one the rule would show, or there is a hole.
  - **Measure a look that streams in only once it is in**: `--far-wait` (and `--hold-wait`), `--settle N` for N frames after, and read the "settled" line; `tools/look_cost.sh --hold-wait --far-wait --settle 2500` does. A `--frames` run that ends as the last tile lands measures one frame (I did, and chased a quarter millisecond that was not there). A recording given `--far-wait` begins only when the far view is whole. **A scripted run without `--frames` or `--record` never ends**: one of mine stood open on his desktop for ten minutes.
  - **The cost is fragments, not vertices** (0.0 to 0.5 ms as a rule, 1.06 at the lake looking across; half of that without multisampling; sending only the faces that look toward the eye saved 0.00 to 0.06). Weigh a rendering technique by which of the two it saves.
  - **`--no-lod` must stay `main`'s picture to the pixel** (still views, clock stopped, no shadows, `--no-whole-map` both sides): the far plane is a variable for that (`Castle.Render.Far_Plane`, the same handed to `Castle.Camera.View_Proj`).
  - The streaming is the game's (`Far_Stream`): the last level over the whole map always, the others by their rings with a margin either side; a tile edited is made again once it shows (`Far_Touch` beside every `Remesh` an edit causes; a load marks all). Anything new that changes blocks tells it there.
  - **A dot of the sky's colour in the land is a hole: count them before calling a far look done** (John: "the white dot dithering for the LOD rings"; 108 pixels of one colour along the first ring in one view). Two levels' fragments of one pixel do not stand in one place on the ground, so any rule that hands a pixel from one level to another must overlap (`Far_Lap`: the coarser has all its pixels before the finer gives up its last), never abut. What is left shows the floor (`Ground_Floor`: dark ground drawn by the sky's pass where the land leaves a pixel open over the map), not the sky. Count with the haze and the floor off (`--no-ground-haze --no-floor`), pixels of the exact horizon colour, still and in a recorded pan.
  - **The sun's shadows past the view** (`Far_Shadows`): two depth maps over the whole map for far ground (four taps each, by the place and never by the level), and the far tiles in the light's way drawn into the near maps as well (`Cast_Tiles` in `Draw`: the ring's rule with no cross-fade, one place). **What casts from beyond a map goes into that map; don't ask a second map on every pixel of the view** (the first version did, to find far casters: 0.4 to 0.9 ms; this way is free). A far map is drawn again when its direction moves, else not more often than every 20 frames (300 for the stacks alone or a tile made again), one in a frame. `tools/shadow_still.sh` must still read 0.00 %, **and its fast-clock setting is run three times, not once**: with one tap to a far map it read 0.00 % the first time and 0.94 % and 1.11 % after (the edge crept between the two held maps, whose texels do not lie alike on the ground; four taps hold it), and I had quoted the first. **Count and measure again after the last change, not before it**: the hole counts and the stillness in the first draft of the handoff were from before two later commits.
  - **The ground haze is in `air`** (`Ground_Haze`): how deep it lies is a texture worked out once from the generator's map (1 m on level ground and on water, less on steep ground, none where the generator's mountain mask is 0.2 or more; no gully filling since the third round: "remove the fingers and haze off the mountains"), and a thing is in it by how far it stands over the land as made (`u_height`, declared once in the shared sky source now). **A tile of the far view is forgiven half its cell there (`g_slack`, set by whoever calls `air`)**: anything else in `air` or the land's shader that goes by a fragment's height must do the same, or far land is striped, a band to every cell of height. Its thickness goes by the distance from the focus or from under the eye, never by the angle looked through.
  - **Measure a look in wet weather as well as dry**: the far shadows cost 0.01 to 0.03 ms in dry weather and 0.18 in rain (the wet program's; why was not found). **And say a throttle counted in frames in seconds at sixty a second**: every cost run here is at a thousand frames a second with vsync off, where 20 frames is nothing; in his hands it is a third of a second.
  - **To compare two builds' pictures pixel by pixel: `--workers 1 --timescale 0 --no-whole-map --drawn-still`** (one worker so the stacks arrive in one order, the sun stopped, drawn time held still; a build before 2026-11-16 has no `--drawn-still`: patch a scratch copy of it). Without them a build differs from itself by thousands of pixels and the comparison says nothing.
  - **The old far land is out of the game** (his word: "remove the old far land, but keep the functionality just comment it out or something, just in case"): `--far` is commented out in the game (two marked lines bring it back), nothing of it is made unless `Castle.Render.Far_Land` is set before `Init`, and all of it still compiles, with `edit_check` proving `Castle.Far`. Don't delete it and don't mend it unasked.

- Touch-ups and a stress test before stage 6: the sky, mist, wet surfaces, lights, the moon (2026-11-11; `docs/handoffs/Handoff_2026_11_11_sky_and_weather.md` has his words, what was built and his calls). Accepted as built: on `main`. His answer settled two things: **multisampling stays** (don't build the single-sample picture unasked) and **no budget of time for the colony's steps** (don't add one unasked). First values, his to tune; don't treat one as settled against him, and don't change one unasked. In the code:
  - **The sky is put together in one order** wherever it is drawn or mirrored (the shared `Sky_Source`): `sky_back` (the gradient), `orbs` (the sun and the moon: discs that only ever add light, never mixed in at the colour of the light on the land, which is what made the sun's disc dark under cloud), then clouds. A new thing in the sky goes into that order, once.
  - **Where the sun is and what lights the land are two names**: `u_sun` (and `u_sun_glow`, `u_sun_disc`, `u_moon`) for the sky, `u_lamp` and `u_lamp_col` for shading, shadows, the clouds' shadows and glints. The lamp is the sun until its light is gone and then the moon, by one routine (`Lamp_At`) that the daylight and the shadows' held directions both use; the one's light is nothing where the other takes over. Never light the land from `u_sun`, and never change lamps where the strength is not zero (`tools/shadow_still.sh` through a dusk must read 0.00 %).
  - **The moon's phase is the clock's** (`Castle.Climate.Moon_Phase`: ten days, full on day 20.5): nothing saved, nothing the colony knows. The night's own light (`Sky_Amb` at night) is as it was, so a moonless night is no darker than nights were: his to change.
  - **Stars are drawn by the sky pass only**, behind the clouds, never in the shared `sky()` (the water's mirror would pay for them every fragment). They turn with the clock, so a starry sky is the one thing in a still view that is not still.
  - **Only the clouds' pass walks** (`Cloud_Source`, `Cloud_Program`: half size, run only when sky above the horizon is in the view). `sky`, `haze` and `cloud_shade` run for every fragment of the land, the figures and the water and must stay a tap or two; they take the weather's flat map, which is also where the walked clouds stand, so a shadow lies under its cloud. Don't move the one without the other. Nothing in the sky may change from frame to frame but the drift (the walk's start is offset by the pixel, never by the frame).
  - **The camera sees the sky only to about 20 degrees up** (`Min_Pitch`), so the clouds were tuned for two to fifteen kilometres off and the moon is out of the picture most of the night. Look again when the on-foot view comes.
  - **The air is one routine**, `air` in `Sky_Source`: the haze at the edge of the loaded world, the weather's murk and mist. Anything else that lies between the eye and a thing goes there. Mist's amount is `Castle.Climate.Mist`, a function of the clock, the weather and the wet state: keep it one, with nothing saved, and unknown to the colony.
  - **Measure a new look switched off as well as on**, against `main` built beside it. Mist and the lights are tests in the one shader and cost nothing when there is none; the wet look behind `if (u_wet > 0.0)` cost 0.1 ms in dry weather, never run (why was not found), so the land's program is built twice, without it and with it (`World_Fragment (Wet)`, `#ifdef WET`, `Use_World`). That is the way out when a look costs while it is off.
  - **Inside a branch that not every pixel of a face takes, read textures at a given detail** (`textureLod`; `sky_at` is `sky` that way).
  - **Lights are a table** (`Clear_Lights`, `Add_Light`, each frame; `Place_Lights` culls to the 64 nearest the focus and writes which reach each 16 m of the world). `firelight` sums a fragment's own cell's. The nearest casts; making others cast is the open decision for the first hearth indoors.
  - **Drawn time is `Sky_T`** in the game (clouds, ripples, falling rain), stepped a thirtieth of a second a frame in a recording like `Flame_T`. Don't feed the wall clock to anything a recording shows.

- The last pass before stage 6: models drawn rounded, the bow and the axe, foliage, the herds' flight, the fringe of paths (2026-11-11; `docs/handoffs/Handoff_2026_11_11_models_pass.md` has his words, what was built and his calls). Accepted as built: on `main`. The values are first values: his to tune, don't treat one as settled against him, and don't change one unasked. In the code:
  - **Foliage's softness and the fringe are drawn, not made.** No block changed for them: the mesher marks (`Castle.Mesher`: soft quads for the edges and corners of a clump of leaves, eight fringe bits on a top face of turf or bare earth) and the shaders draw. Anything in the world shader that needs the block or the texel takes it from `v_grid` (where the vertex is on the block grid), never from `v_world` (where foliage was drawn to): the shadow fragment too. A corner of the block grid must be moved the same way by every face that meets it, in every pass, or crowns gape: by place alone (`vhash`), never by time (the sun's shadows are held: `tools/shadow_still.sh` must still read 0.00 %). A corner on a stack's edge is not moved (the mesher does not know the neighbour's trees). Don't move the flat sides of a clump again without counting quads (it was 43 % more over the view; as built 15 %). **A boulder is drawn round on the same soft quads** (2026-11-16, "Let's smooth the boulders"; `rendering.md`, "Boulders drawn round"): its hollows drawn out as its edges are drawn in (the outward corners marked in the soft quad's unused w bits), its body the solid blocks only, and on a stack's edge its corners moved by what both stacks work out alike from the generator (`Castle.Flora.In_Rock`, the ground as made): **whatever decides where a shared corner goes must be the same function of the same inputs on both sides of the line**, or a slit opens. A solid face looking at a boulder is meshed (hidden while the boulder is whole) so a corner drawn in never shows a hole. **Generator 15 (the same day, "they need extend into the ground"): a boulder's shape is `Castle.Flora.Rock_Column`**, column by column on each column's own ground (filled under, a skirt at the foot); the generator (`Draw_Rock`) and the mesher's stack-edge corners (`In_Rock`) both go through it: keep it one function. Its foot in the drawing (`Boulder_Feet`, z code 3) only ever goes out and down. In the code:
  - **What casts is one routine**, `Draw_Casters` in `castle-render.adb`: the stacks' solid quads and every box `Castle.Figures` was given this frame, into any light's depth map (the sun's, the fire's). So a new block, prop, animal or thing carried casts with nothing more to do; done-test: a new prop has no shadow code of its own. Three things cast nothing on purpose: grass, grain and wild vegetables (`u_plants`), a box that glows (`Box.Glow`), a look made with `New_Look (..., Casts => False)`. A new way of drawing solid things (neither a stack nor a figure) gets its depth pass in `Draw_Casters`, once.
  - **Never draw a sun map for where the sun is.** The maps are held at two directions and mixed (`Hold_Sun`, `Shadow_Hold`, `Shadow_Min_Frames`); a map redrawn as the sun moves is what flickered. After touching the maps, the lookups (`Sunlit_Source`) or the sun, run `tools/shadow_still.sh` (needs a window, numpy and Pillow): "pixels stepping >6" should read 0.00 % with the clock running, at `--timescale 0.05` and at `--time 18.5` (0.01 % was seen once, with the clock stopped, as the camera settled: run it again before believing a stray hundredth; before the maps were held it read 5.8 %). Shadows are meant to hold still under the sun; the fire's are meant to stir (its light wanders).
  - `Castle.Render.GPU_Millis` covers the whole frame, the shadow passes too (it began after them until 2026-11-10: older figures in the handoffs leave them out).
  - **A fire is `Castle.Fire`**: `Show (bed, heat, time, wind)` draws flames, sparks and smoke as small pieces through `Castle.Props`; `Light_At` and `Light_Strength` feed `Castle.Render.Add_Light`. It is of the figures' layer (it draws through props; the renderer does not know it). It is a pure function of place, heat and the drawn time (`Flame_T`, which a recording steps): keep no state in it and save nothing of it. Another fire is another `Show` and another `Add_Light`: the lights are a table since 2026-11-11 (`Clear_Lights`, `Add_Light`, 64 in a view; `docs/systems/rendering.md`, "Lights"); the one nearest the focus casts, the others shine through what stands in their way. The pieces rise, narrow and cool; none turns or bobs in place (that was the old look he called unnatural).
